Data & sources

These visualisations examine the five research questions using supplied BITRE hospitalisation data. The explanations summarise the displayed patterns and the limits of each comparison.

Read the preliminary investigation ↗
How are averages calculated?

Averages use the displayed observations: all 11 years for annual trends, displayed groups for comparison views, and published jurisdictions for Q2. Q4 calculates a separate average for each displayed sex across age groups. Q5 averages the displayed age-by-road-user changes. E1 excludes years with no matching cases when calculating mean shares. Each chart labels its own mean and period; calculations use full precision.

How should comparisons be interpreted?

These are counts and shares, rather than population-standardised or exposure-adjusted risks. Age bands have unequal widths. The map labels suppressed values as not published. Jurisdiction totals can differ from national totals because of unrecorded or migratory/offshore residence. Q4 compares male and female counts; Other / not recorded cases are disclosed separately and are not included in the mirrored comparison. Bed days measure hospital resource use and are not a direct measure of injury severity.

The source notes a series break for Victoria from 2012 and New South Wales from June 2017. These changes should be considered when interpreting national trends.

Road-user share of national hospitalisations (%) · 2011–2021 · Source: BITRE

CHART ANALYSIS

The cyclist share of hospitalisations rose from 15.8% in 2011 to 21.9% in 2020, before falling to 20.7% in 2021. The 2021 share remained above both 2011 and 2019, when it was 18.3%.

Over the same period, the vehicle-occupant share fell from approximately 52.0% to 49.4%, while the motorcyclist share remained around 22–23%. A change in share reflects a group's position within the annual total and does not necessarily mean its count changed in the same direction.

Shares of hospitalisations and bed days (%) · 2016–2021 · Source: BITRE

CHART ANALYSIS

During 2016–2021, cyclists accounted for 19.02% of hospitalisations but 11.13% of bed days. Pedestrians accounted for 6.49% of hospitalisations and 13.14% of bed days, while motorcyclists accounted for 22.27% and 25.02% respectively.

A higher bed-day share than hospitalisation share indicates a longer average hospital stay relative to the overall average. Bed days measure hospital resource use; they do not provide a complete measure of injury severity because length of stay also depends on treatment, other health conditions, and discharge arrangements.

CHART ANALYSIS

During 2016–2021, people aged 40–64 accounted for the largest shares of hospitalisations (32.82%) and bed days (34.17%). People aged 65–74 accounted for 7.31% of hospitalisations and 11.18% of bed days; those aged 75 and over accounted for 6.23% and 13.29% respectively.

The larger bed-day shares among older groups indicate longer average hospital stays relative to the overall average. By comparison, people aged 8–16 accounted for 6.61% of hospitalisations and 3.53% of bed days. These differences describe hospital resource use and should not be treated as direct measures of injury severity.
