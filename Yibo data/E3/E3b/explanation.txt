From 2016 to 2021, people aged 40–64 accounted for the largest shares of both hospitalisations (32.82%) and bed days (34.17%). However, older age groups showed a disproportionate bed-day burden. People aged 75 and over represented only 6.23% of hospitalisations but 13.29% of bed days, while those aged 65–74 accounted for 7.31% of hospitalisations but 11.18% of bed days. In contrast, people aged 8–16 represented 6.61% of hospitalisations but only 3.53% of bed days. These results suggest that hospitalisations among older adults tend to consume more hospital resources relative to their case numbers. Bed days indicate healthcare resource use, but they should not be interpreted as a complete measure of injury severity because length of stay may also be affected by treatment needs, comorbidities, discharge arrangements, and other factors.
