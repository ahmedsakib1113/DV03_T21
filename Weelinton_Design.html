Commentary

The 2011–2021 remoteness slopegraph shows that 69.2% of Non-Indigenous injuries occurred in Major Cities, compared to only 2.1% in Remote areas. In contrast, 65.7% of First Nations injuries occurred in Regional (39.8%) and Remote (25.9%) areas.

The road-user breakdown in the 100% stacked bar chart further highlights these differences. First Nations peoples have higher proportions of car occupant (54.3% vs. 48.2%) and pedestrian injuries (12.1% vs. 6.8%), but a lower proportion of pedal cyclist injuries (11.1% vs. 19.0%) compared to Non-Indigenous Australians. This suggests that road safety concerns for First Nations communities are more closely linked to car travel and pedestrian exposure, particularly in regional and remote areas.
